\label{ch:receiver}
\index{receiver}\index{SEFD}

The class \texttt{interferosim.instrument.Receiver} holds the system
temperature and the sampling parameters of the SDR; together with an
antenna it gives the system equivalent flux density and the radiometer
noise. The device profile of the Ettus USRP B210 is
\texttt{configs/receivers/b210.toml} (settings of notebook~B); unknown
keys of the file are kept in \texttt{extra}.

The AD9361 resets the phase between the two channels on every retune:
tune once, then calibrate the fixed offset with a common noise source.

\paragraph*{Receive path.} Fig.~\ref{fig:b210-block} shows the signal path
of one station: external LNA, the AD9361 (gain, quadrature mixer to
complex baseband, low-pass filter, 12-bit ADC), the FPGA (decimation,
time stamps) and the host, which records the IQ samples. LO and sample
clock follow the reference -- the internal TCXO or a GPS-disciplined
oscillator with PPS.

\paragraph*{Derived quantities.} \texttt{Receiver} computes from them
\begin{align}
  T_\mathrm{rx} &= 290\,\mathrm{K}\,\bigl(10^{\mathrm{NF}/10}-1\bigr)
     && \texttt{receiver\_noise\_temperature\_k()}\\
  R &= f_s \cdot n_\mathrm{bytes} \cdot n_\mathrm{ch}
     && \texttt{data\_rate\_bytes\_per\_s()}\\
  \Delta f_\mathrm{LO} &= y\,f_c
     && \texttt{lo\_offset\_hz()}\\
  t_1 &= \frac{1}{2\pi\,y\,f_c}
     && \texttt{phase\_coherence\_time\_s()}
\end{align}
with the relative frequency error $y$ of the reference chosen by
\texttt{clock\_source}. With NF = 8\,dB the B210 alone has
$T_\mathrm{rx}\approx 1540\,\mathrm{K}$; the system temperature of 120\,K
of the notebooks therefore requires a low-noise amplifier at the antenna
(\texttt{external\_lna = true}). $t_1$ uses the data-sheet
\emph{accuracy}; the short-term stability of a locked GPSDO is better, so
$t_1$ is a lower limit. \texttt{validate()} returns warnings where a setting
exceeds the device: sample rate or bandwidth above the maximum for the
number of channels, bandwidth above the sample rate, centre frequency
outside the RF range, gain outside its range, $T_\mathrm{sys} < T_\mathrm{rx}$
without external LNA.

\section{Sensitivity}

\begin{align}
  \mathrm{SEFD} &= \frac{2kT_\mathrm{sys}}{A_\mathrm{eff}}
     && \texttt{sefd\_jy(antenna, f)}\\
  \sigma_S &= \frac{\mathrm{SEFD}}{\sqrt{B\tau}}
     && \texttt{single\_dish\_sigma\_jy(antenna, t, f)}\\
  \sigma_V &= \frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{SEFD}_1\,\mathrm{SEFD}_2}}{\sqrt{2B\tau}}
     && \texttt{baseline\_sigma\_jy(a1, a2, t, f)}
\end{align}
$\sigma_V$ is the noise of the real and of the imaginary part of one
visibility (convention of \texttt{aas6sim}). For the B210 profile
($B = 2\,\mathrm{MHz}$) and $\tau = 10\,\mathrm{s}$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
Array & baseline & $\sigma_V$ (10\,s) & $|V|/\sigma_V$: Sun, Cyg A \\
\midrule
two dishes 3\,m (AAS6 simulation) & 11.09\,km & 12.4\,Jy & 1.2, 1.7 \\
two horns 15\,dBi (notebook B) & 10\,m & 467\,Jy & 752, 3.3 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
On the long baseline the sources are largely resolved (chapter
\enquote{Sky Models}); on the short one the Sun dominates by far. The
notebook \texttt{02\_instrument.ipynb} computes the table for all
sky models.
