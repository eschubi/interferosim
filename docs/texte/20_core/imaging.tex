\label{ch:imaging}
\index{imaging}\index{dirty beam}\index{weighting}

Gridding, natural/uniform/Wiener weighting, dirty image and dirty beam
(PSF). Implemented in version~0.7.0 in the package
\texttt{interferosim.imaging} (classes as in the basic design,
Fig.~\ref{fig:uml-pipeline}); notebook \texttt{06\_imaging.ipynb}. Origin:
notebook~A of the teaching material (case~B, the sparsely sampled
interferometer). Theory: chapter \enquote{Imaging} in part
\enquote{Theory}.

\section{Implementation}

\begin{description}
  \item[\texttt{Gridder(npix, cell\_arcsec)}] nearest-cell gridding on an
        $N\times N$ uv-grid, cell $\Delta u = 1/(N\,\Delta l)$. Every visibility
        enters together with its Hermitian conjugate
        $V(-u,-v) = V^*(u,v)$, weighted with its data weight $1/\sigma^2$;
        channels are gridded at their own frequency (multi-frequency
        synthesis). An optional shift of the phase centre,
        $V' = V\,e^{+2\pi i(ul_0+vm_0)}$, puts the offset $(l_0,m_0)$ into the
        image centre. Visibilities outside the grid are counted.
  \item[Weighting] (strategy pattern) per cell from the gridded weight
        $W$: \texttt{NaturalWeighting} (factor 1 -- the data weights),
        \texttt{UniformWeighting} ($1/W$ -- every occupied cell counts once),
        \texttt{WienerWeighting(k)} ($W/(W^2+K)$, $K = k\,\langle W\rangle^2$;
        notebook~A: for a sparsely sampled interferometer the Wiener filter
        only re-weights the measured cells).
  \item[\texttt{Imager}] inverse FFT of the weighted grid (dirty image) and
        of the weight grid (dirty beam), normalised so that the beam peaks
        at 1 and a point source shows its flux density in Jy/beam. Convention
        as \texttt{SkyModel.visibility}:
        $V = \sum S\,e^{-2\pi i(ul+vm)} \Rightarrow
         I_D = \sum w\,V\,e^{+2\pi i(ul+vm)} = I * B_D$.
  \item[\texttt{Image}] map with pixel size in arcsec, axes relative to the
        image centre; peak, value at a position, rms outside a circle, FWHM of
        the main lobe.
\end{description}

\section{Results}

\paragraph*{OH masers with the project baseline.} Three masers of 70, 45
and 20\,Jy within $15''$, 1665\,MHz, $\pm 8$\,h on the 11.5\,km baseline: the
single ellipse in the uv-plane gives a dirty beam of concentric fringes with
a main lobe of $1.8''\times1.6''$ and sidelobes up to 0.46
(Fig.~\ref{fig:img-masers}). Each maser appears as a copy of the beam; the
sidelobes of each fall on the others (M1: 62 instead of 70\,Jy/beam at its
position) -- the task of deconvolution.

\paragraph*{Weighting.} Natural weighting gives the lowest noise and the
widest main lobe, uniform the sharpest main lobe and lower sidelobes at
higher noise; the Wiener weighting lies in between, set by $k$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:img-weighting}).

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
Weighting & main lobe (FWHM) & highest sidelobe & noise [Jy/beam] \\
\midrule
natural          & $1.78''\times1.60''$ & 0.46 & 0.15 \\
Wiener, $k = 1$    & $1.74''\times1.63''$ & 0.45 & 0.16 \\
Wiener, $k = 0.01$ & $1.55''\times1.77''$ & 0.32 & 0.20 \\
uniform          & $1.55''\times1.77''$ & 0.33 & 0.21 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph*{Cygnus~A.} Baselines of 6--13\,km sample only 30--60\,k$\lambda$:
the compact hotspots appear, the lobes ($45''\times25''$) are resolved out
(Fig.~\ref{fig:img-cyga}). Without the zero spacing the integral of the dirty
image vanishes; bright features sit in negative bowls. Short baselines (or a
single-dish map) would be needed for the extended emission.
